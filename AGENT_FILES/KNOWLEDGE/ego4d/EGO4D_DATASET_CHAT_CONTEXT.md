Handoff: Dataset migration decision for the JEPA-flow video world model
Project context
The system is a hierarchical JEPA-flow video world model. A frozen V-JEPA / DINOv2 ViT encoder produces detailed latents, a trainable bottleneck compresses them into an abstract latent, and coarse and fine flow-matching predictors learn to predict future latents against a stop-gradient EMA target branch. Training is in latent space, not pixel space. Phase 1 is building the representation and proving the coarse predictor beats trivial baselines. The longer-term goal is a world action model, meaning an action-conditioned architecture, so dataset and viewpoint choices now have downstream consequences.
Current dataset is Something-Something V2 (SSv2), stored on a RunPod network volume. The dataloader is a pure glob of *.webm files with zero label or class dependency, which makes it highly portable across datasets.
The problem that started the search
SSv2 is temporally rich but visually simple. Backgrounds are plain, motion is small-object manipulation on a static camera, resolution is 240p at 12fps. The concrete failure it drives is the copy-ratio problem: coarse_vs_copy_ratio fails because present and future are nearly identical in embedding space.
The mechanism, which the next conversation should keep in mind, is this. The frozen encoder is appearance-dominated. When the camera is static and only a small object moves, the unchanged background dominates the embedding, so the current abstract latent and the future abstract latent land almost on top of each other. Copying the present becomes a near-optimal predictor of the future, the coarse predictor cannot beat it, and it collapses to learning an identity template with no gradient signal for real dynamics.
The fix is a dataset that produces large, structured, learnable divergence between present and future latents. Large enough that copying fails, but structured enough that prediction still beats a batch-mean baseline. Egocentric head motion delivers this most reliably, because it shifts the entire frame every step while staying physically continuous.
Datasets considered and why each was eliminated
We evaluated a wide set against these criteria: fixes the copy-ratio problem, is diverse across domains rather than single-domain, aligns with the eventual world action model, is easy to migrate onto the existing volume, and is reasonably in-distribution for the frozen V-JEPA encoder.
Rejected:
SSv2 is the incumbent being replaced. Too visually simple, which is the root cause of the copy-ratio failure.
Kinetics-700 is the easiest migration (short trimmed clips, direct CVDF and Academic Torrents mirrors at 256p, no chunker, no license) and is literally in V-JEPA's pretraining mix. But it is exocentric, editorially trimmed to show one action, and appearance shortcuts mean present often approximately equals future. It is weak on the exact problem we most need solved and weak on the action-model goal. Not primary.
EPIC-KITCHENS-100 is egocentric and fixes copy-ratio well, but it is single-domain. Training on it yields a kitchen world model. Rejected as primary on that basis. It was briefly floated as a cheap egocentric proving ground, then dropped because the single-domain limitation defeats the purpose.
BDD100K and nuScenes are strong on temporal dynamics but single-domain driving.
AVA has the richest cinematic scenes, but it is built from movies with shot cuts. Cuts make the future unpredictable, which fixes the copy baseline but breaks the batch-mean baseline. Wrong kind of divergence.
Assembly101 is the densest hand-object dataset, but the domain is narrow (toy assembly), 8 of its 12 cameras are static rig views that reintroduce the fixed-background problem, and videos are long-form.
HowTo100M is diverse, hand-object heavy, and in V-JEPA's pretraining mix, but it is long-form and "produced," so it carries both chunking cost and shot-cut contamination. It is a worse version of Panda-70M.
Moments in Time is diverse and uniform at 3 seconds, but it is registration-gated and 3 seconds may be too short for the horizon.
The structural constraint that decided it
You cannot get all three of: diverse across domains, egocentric, and no license wait. This is structural, not incidental. Every large diverse egocentric dataset (Ego4D, Ego-Exo4D, EPIC) is license-gated because egocentric capture records bystanders and homes, so privacy review is mandatory. The choice is therefore a pick-two:
Keep diverse and egocentric, drop no-license: land on Ego4D or Ego-Exo4D.
Keep diverse and no-license, drop egocentric: land on web video (Panda-70M or Kinetics), which is mostly exocentric and a gamble on the copy-ratio fix, since a large fraction of web clips (news, talking heads, product shots) are low-motion and collapse present into future again.
Drop diverse: back to the single-domain options already rejected.
The V-JEPA logic check
We tested whether the candidates fit the reasoning behind V-JEPA's own data choices. V-JEPA 2 pretrained on SSv2, Kinetics 400/600/700, HowTo100M, YT-Temporal-1B (general YouTube), and ImageNet. By literal membership, none of the finalists are in it, and Panda-70M is closest in spirit because it is the general-web-video role that YT-Temporal-1B played.
But that is the pretraining logic, which optimizes a general encoder. Our project is at the predictor and world-model stage, where the V-JEPA team made different choices: V-JEPA 2-AC, the action-conditioned world model, was post-trained on DROID robot trajectories, and V-JEPA 2.1 is evaluated on Ego4D short-term object-interaction anticipation and EPIC-KITCHENS action anticipation. So when the V-JEPA team built predictors and planners, they reached for egocentric and embodied data, not the pretraining mix. That validates both the egocentric direction and the eventual passive-to-action-conditioned path.
On encoder compatibility specifically: even though Ego4D is not in pretraining, V-JEPA 2.1 hits state-of-the-art on Ego4D and EPIC with frozen features, which means egocentric video is effectively in-distribution for the frozen encoder. Panda-70M looks right by pretraining membership but reintroduces the motion-poverty that the V-JEPA team deliberately compensated for by mixing in SSv2, and we have no such motion component to pair it with.
Decision
Primary choice: Ego4D. It fixes copy-ratio reliably through egocentric motion, it is genuinely multi-domain (unscripted daily life across 74 locations), it is the best human-video bridge to the action model (first-person viewpoint plus hand-object interaction, and a forecasting-hands-and-objects benchmark), and the frozen encoder handles it well. The two-day license approval is the only real cost, and it is calendar wait that runs in parallel with building the pipeline, so it costs roughly zero active engineering time. Sign the license first, build during approval.
Commit to one dataset, not two. Specifically do not pair with Kinetics to fill the wait, because Kinetics reintroduces exocentric appearance-shortcut content that would muddy the copy-ratio diagnosis. Prove the hierarchy on one clean egocentric source first.
Alternative if weighting skilled manipulation over daily-life breadth: Ego-Exo4D. It is 1,286 hours of egocentric plus exocentric skilled activity across eight domains (cooking, bike repair, music, basketball, and others), with paired views. Same two-day license and chunking cost as Ego4D, slightly less daily-life diversity, richer manipulation depth.
Fallback only if signing the license is a hard no: Panda-70M, filtered to its dynamic and hand-interaction subset (its captions allow keyword filtering, and its semantic splitting preserves within-clip continuity so there are no internal cuts). This accepts curation work and a partial copy-ratio gamble as the price of staying license-free. It beats Kinetics in this branch for those two reasons.
Migration specifics already worked out
The volume keeps datasets as siblings under /workspace/data/, with raw files under a _raw directory and symlink or chunk directories under data/<dataset>/{train,validation}/. The plan mirrors the existing ssv2_raw to data/ssv2 relationship with a new ego4d_raw to data/ego4d.
The critical technical issue is fps and horizon. SSv2 is 12fps; the dataloader uses t_ctx=8, frame_stride=2, horizon_k=4, which gives a real prediction horizon of about 0.33 seconds. Ego4D is 30fps, so leaving the config unchanged would compress the horizon to about 0.13 seconds, at which nothing has moved and the copy-ratio problem gets worse by construction. Two resolutions:
Option A, recommended: re-encode chunks to 12fps (and downscale the shorter side to 256) during chunking. Config stays byte-identical to SSv2, and the SSv2-calibrated thresholds carry over. Heavier one-time preprocessing, lighter decode every epoch.
Option B: keep 30fps native via stream-copy chunking, and set frame_stride=5 (config-only, no CLI flag) and horizon_k=10 (via --horizon-k). Fast preprocessing, heavier recurring decode.
Option A is recommended but was left for final confirmation.
Chunking plan: Ego4D videos are hours long, so chunk into roughly 4-second clips with a sliding window. Split by source video, not by chunk, to prevent train/val leakage. Target about 169k train chunks to match SSv2-full, which needs roughly 188 to 210 source hours. Download only that subset via the Ego4D CLI using video_540ss and a UID file, after the license approves. Budget on the order of 100 to 200 GB; verify volume free space first.
Code deltas needed, all additive and leaving SSv2 paths untouched:

Generalize the dataloader glob from *.webm to also match *.mp4.
Add Ego4D roots and dataset_root() branches in the config, mirroring the ssv2 and ssv2_tiny pattern.
Add ego4d and ego4d_tiny to the --data CLI choices.
New script to select source-video UIDs totaling the target hours from the Ego4D manifest.
New chunker script that segments long videos, splits by source video, and applies the Option A or Option B fps handling.

Nothing in the model, losses, EMA logic, or training loop changes. The frozen encoder consumes the same clip tensor shape regardless of dataset.
