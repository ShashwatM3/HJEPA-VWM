  and processed data sit on disk at the same moment.

  The point is that the raw downloads are just an intermediate product. A raw
  540p 30fps EGO4D video only exists on your volume so ffmpeg can read it once
  and write out its 4-second chunks — after that it's dead weight. So:

  - One-shot: download all 290 GB of intermediates → chunk everything → delete
  all 290 GB. For a window of several hours, the volume holds all the raw and
  all the chunks simultaneously. Peak ≈ 460 GB.
  - Batched: split the ~525 source videos into 4 groups. Download group 1 (~75
  GB) → chunk it → delete group 1's raw → download group 2 → ... The chunks
  accumulate to the same final ~90 GB either way, but you never hold more than
  ~75 GB of raw at once. Peak ≈ 230 GB.

  An analogy: you're translating four boxes of books and discarding the
  originals when done. You can haul all four boxes into your office at once
  (need a big office), or bring one box at a time and return each when finished
  (small office, same translated library at the end).

  The trade-off is purely operational: the batched path means running the
  download→chunk→delete loop four times instead of once — same total machine
